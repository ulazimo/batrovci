### 1.3 Decisions taken

- **Renderer** — 2D physics in sheet-space, Canvas 2D rendering through a perspective projection. The playfield is genuinely flat, so a projective transform is visually equivalent to a 3D camera at a fraction of the cost. Rocks and brushes are depth-scaled sprites generated by Layer AI. No build step, no 3D library — consistent with the rest of this prototype lab.
- **Opponent** — local hot-seat only, per the doc. You play Yellow, then Red. No placeholder bot; Milestone 9 would replace it.

### 1.3a Revised during the build

One design assumption did not survive contact with the numbers, and it is worth recording
because it shaped the whole look.

**A textbook perspective camera is unplayable here.** The plan assumed a single projective
transform with screen row and width sharing one 1/distance falloff — a real pinhole camera.
Built and measured, the house is 3.66 m across and 38 m from the hack, which works out to
about **7 vertical pixels** on a phone. Correct, and you cannot tell which ring a rock is in.
Worse, no choice of lens fixes it: the vertical scale at the house is maximised at one
particular eye distance and even that peak is ~8 px.

So depth and width were decoupled and each got its own control. Depth stays close to even, so
a metre near the camera covers roughly the same pixels as a metre at the house — distances
stay fair to judge and the house is legible. The perspective read comes almost entirely from
the width taper, which costs nothing in readability. That combination is what the Curling
Sheet reference diagram and the shooting-perspective photo actually look like, and with the
Layer AI rock shading on top it reads as the stylized 3D the art direction asks for.

Two consequences worth knowing:
- The shooting view and the House view want *opposite* framing — the shooting view crops the
  side lines so rocks read big, the House view must show full width or a rock on the edge of
  the twelve-foot is clipped off-screen. Both the depth span and the width scale are therefore
  per-camera-mode, and tween together on Focus House.
- `projSheetFill` (move the camera closer) is the right lever when rocks look small.
  `rockRadiusScale` changes how many rocks fit in the house, which is a strategy change — in
  real curling 16 rocks genuinely do not fit, and that is the whole game.

### 1.4 Assumptions to flag

Three places where the Initial Version chapter list and the rest of the doc need reconciling. I have picked the reading that makes the Initial Version playable and noted it here rather than guessing silently:

1. **Local Multiplayer is the play mode.** The chapter list does not name *Local Multiplayer*, but that chapter states it "will be the initial version of the game, since Bot Opponent and Multiplayer will require more development," and Bot Opponent sits at Milestone 9. With no bot and no netcode, hot-seat is the only way an End can be completed. Building it.
2. **A minimal coin toss, not the Milestone screen.** *Hammer Rules* is in scope and says End 1's Hammer "is determined by a Coin before the match," but the richer pre-match flow (opponent avatar, trophy count, coin animation, winner celebration) is flagged *Milestone Bot Opponent/Multiplayer*. Building a lightweight yellow/red coin flip only; deferring the avatar/trophy/celebration screens.
3. **Rock stats exist as data, not as a screen.** The *Rocks (Inventory Screen tab)* chapter is out of scope, but *Rock Launching Controls and Flow* depends on the Accuracy, Curl and Trajectory stats, and *Rock Physics* on Power. Implementing the four 5-level stat ladders and a Basic Rock at the stated levels (Power L3 = reaches the back of the House) as tuning data driving the shot UI. No Inventory UI.

## 2. Geometry — the source of truth

All lengths in metres, taken from WCF R1 and R2. Playing direction is **+Y**, toward the house. `x = 0` is the centre line.

Because Curling Clash never swaps ends, the sheet is asymmetric: one house, at the playing end.

| Landmark | Y (m) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery hack (origin) | 0.000 | Hack line at the end of the centre line |
| Delivery tee line | 3.658 | Centre line extends 12 ft beyond the tee |
| Delivery hog line | 10.059 | Tee → hog = 6.401 m (21 ft) |
| Playing hog line (inside edge) | 32.005 | Hog → hog = 21.946 m (72 ft) |
| **Playing tee line / Button centre** | 38.405 | Tee → tee = 34.747 m (114 ft) |
| Back line (outside edge) | 40.234 | Tee → back = 1.829 m (6 ft) |

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Sheet width (side line to side line) | 4.750 m → `x ∈ [-2.375, +2.375]` |
| House outer radius (12-foot) | 1.829 m |
| 8-foot ring radius | 1.219 m |
| 4-foot ring radius | 0.610 m |
| Button radius | 0.152 m |
| Rock radius | 0.1455 m (circumference ≤ 914 mm) |
| Rock height | 0.114 m |
| Rock mass | 17.24 – 19.96 kg (default 19.0, sliderised) |

**Colours** — house rings from `Curling House Color Reference 1/2.jpeg`: 12-ft and 8-ft in royal blue, 4-ft and button in red, white between. Handles: Yellow = user, Red = opponent.

**In / out of play** (WCF R2 f, g, h):
- A rock that does not come fully past the inside edge of the playing hog line is removed — *unless* it struck another rock.
- A rock that completely crosses the outside edge of the back line is removed.
- A rock touching a side line is removed.
- Removal plays the swipe-away-to-the-back animation from the doc.

**In the house** — a rock counts if any part of it touches or overlaps the 12-foot circle: `dist(rock, tee) < 1.829 + 0.1455`.

### 3.1 Physics model

Fixed timestep (120 Hz, sub-stepped inside `requestAnimationFrame`) so results are frame-rate independent and reproducible.

Per rock: `{ x, y, vx, vy, spin, mass, radius, team, inPlay, hasStruck }`.

- **Friction / traction** — `a = -(A + B·v)` along the velocity vector. `A` is the Coulomb-ish floor that guarantees a stop; `B` shapes the long glide. Both sliderised, so "traction" is directly tunable as the doc asks. Real stones decelerate non-linearly; this two-term form reproduces the feel with two comprehensible knobs.
- **Curl** — lateral acceleration `a_lat = C · sign(spin) · g(v)`, where `g(v)` rises as speed falls, so the rock curls most at the end of its travel like a real stone. CCW handle → curves right, CW → curves left, per the doc. Total lateral deflection clamped by the rock's Curl stat.
- **Spin decay** — `spin` decays over distance travelled; the handle keeps rotating visually until the rock stops.
- **Collisions** — circle-circle impulse resolution with a restitution slider and a separate energy-loss slider, plus positional de-penetration. Resolved iteratively within a substep so a single shot can chain through several rocks. Sets `hasStruck` (which exempts a rock from the hog-line rule).
- **Brushing** — while held, scales `A` and `B` down inside a zone a tunable distance ahead of the rock, and adds a lateral bias for side-brushing. Ramps in and out; traction returns to normal the moment brushing stops, per the doc.

### 3.2 Shot Area, variance and Trajectory

The doc asks for two things that need care together: the landing point is *"determined when the Shot has been released, with random variance"*, yet the Shot Area *"will become smaller and smaller and more deterministic as the Rock is slowing down."*

Resolution:
1. On release, the aim state (power, direction, curl) is perturbed once by a random offset drawn from the Shot Area disc, whose radius comes from the Accuracy stat. The perturbation goes into the **initial velocity and spin**, so the physics stays honest and collisions remain a genuine consequence.
2. Each frame, `predict.js` fast-forwards a collision-free copy of the rock to its rest point. The Shot Area circle is drawn around that predicted point with a radius that lerps to zero as speed falls. Early on it is wide and roughly where the player aimed; by the end it has collapsed onto the true stopping point.

This gives the promised "becomes deterministic" read-out without ever lying about where the rock will actually go.

**Trajectory** uses the same forward sim from the pre-release aim state, drawing only the first `Trajectory stat` fraction of the path, and fading out after release.

### 3.3 Perspective projection

A single homography maps the sheet quad — `x ∈ [±2.375]`, `y ∈ [camY, camY + visibleDepth]` — onto a screen trapezoid, parameterised by horizon height and near/far width ratio (both sliders). Because every game object sits on the ice plane, one projective transform places everything; rocks and brushes are billboards scaled by depth from the same transform. A top-down debug toggle renders the raw sheet space for verifying geometry.

## 4. Tuning panel

The doc asks for a slider six separate times. Rather than scattering magic numbers, every tunable is declared once in `tuning.js` with a default, range and group; `tuning-panel.js` renders live sliders over the game, persists to `localStorage`, and exports JSON so a tuned set can be committed back as new defaults.

Built in Phase 0 and grown every phase after. Sliders the doc asks for by name are marked ★.

| Group | Tunables |
|---|---|
| Ice | ★ base traction `A`, glide term `B`, traction variance across the sheet |
| Rock | ★ mass, radius scale, spin decay rate, launch power curve |
| Curl | curl coefficient `C`, speed-response shape `g(v)`, max deflection per Curl level |
| Collisions | ★ energy loss, restitution, de-penetration stiffness, substep iterations |
| Brushing | ★ traction multiplier while brushing, ★ side-brush lateral force, ★ max side-brush deflection (degrees), reach ahead of rock, intensity curve, ramp in/out |
| Shot UI | perfect-zone centre and width, straight-line snap strength, perfect-power snap strength, shake threshold and amplitude, shot-area radius per Accuracy level, trajectory fraction per Trajectory level |
| Camera | ★ follow band top/bottom, lerp rate, house-clamp offset, Focus House transition time |
| Projection | horizon height, near/far width ratio, rock sprite depth-scale curve |
| Scoring | measurement-drama threshold, count-up pacing, highlight ring style |

## 5. Phases

Each phase ends at a state you can open in the browser and judge. Sliders for that phase's systems ship with the phase.

### Phase 0 — Foundation
Repo skeleton, `index.html` + `style.css`, module load order, `.claude/launch.json` + `devserver.py`, desktop device-frame switcher (iPhone 15 Pro / Galaxy S24+ / Pixel 8) per lab convention, back-link to `../index.html`, `analytics.js` wired to the lab's existing pattern, registration in the root `index.html` projects array with `GAME_ID = 'curling-clash'`, `tuning.js` + `tuning-panel.js` scaffold, `CLAUDE.md`.
**Gate:** app boots in a device frame on the preview server; empty tuning panel opens.

### Phase 1 — Sheet and perspective renderer
`config.js` geometry constants, `projection.js`, `sheet.js`. Draws sheet, side lines, centre line, hog / tee / back lines, hacks, and the four house circles in reference colours. Top-down debug toggle with a metre grid.
**Gate:** sheet is verifiably at official proportions in both perspective and top-down; horizon and width-ratio sliders reshape the view.

### Phase 2 — Rock physics core
`rock.js`, `physics.js`, `predict.js`, plus `debug/physics-bench.html`. Friction, curl, spin decay, chained collisions, all three removal rules, swipe-away animation.
**Gate:** the bench prints a stop-distance table showing Level-3 Power at perfect release reaching the back of the House; curl deflection and collision transfer behave sensibly; all physics sliders live.

### Phase 3 — Shot controls and aiming
`shot.js`. Tap-drag on the rock reveals the power slider; horizontal drag steers direction; semi-transparent fill runs orange → yellow → green through the perfect zone, then orange → red with shake above it; light snapping to the straight line and to perfect power. Curl handle to the right of the rock. Shot Area circle and partial Trajectory. Release resolves variance, fades the trajectory, collapses the shot area. Focus House button on the right in line with the rock; red Back button while focused, with shooting disabled from that view.
**Gate:** a single shot from aim to rest feels good and reads clearly.

### Phase 4 — Brushing
`brushing.js`, `brushes.js`. Thumbstick circle at the bottom: hold to brush, up/down = intensity, left/right = side-brush, fully analog. Brushes fly in from the screen sides on launch, dash ahead of the rock, hold station ahead of it, and ride the camera. Traction drops only in the zone ahead and only while held. Side-brushing bends the path a few tunable degrees. After the rock passes the Tee Line and is still moving, control hands to the opponent for a brief window so they can push it out of the House.
**Gate:** brushing measurably changes where a rock stops and which side it drifts to; the opponent window creates a real decision.

### Phase 5 — Camera
`camera.js`. Follow camera keeping the rock inside a configurable screen band; stops following once the House centre reaches screen centre; smooth transitions in and out of Focus House; clean handoff between shots.
**Gate:** camera never loses the rock, never overshoots the House, and the band sliders do what they say.

### Phase 6 — Match flow and scoring
`state.js`, `match.js`, `scoring.js`. End = 8 rocks per team alternating with the Hammer team last. Coin toss for End 1, then the Hammer transfers to the team that did not score, staying put on a blank End. 3 / 5 / 7 End toggle. Sudden Death End(s), repeating while blank. Live leader indicator with the scoring rocks highlighted in team colour — and nothing highlighted when rocks are on the sheet but none in the House. End-of-End sequence: circle the winning rocks, count up per rock. Measurement drama when the two closest opposing rocks are within the threshold: darken, draw tee-to-nearest-edge lines, resolve with tension. Hot-seat handoff between Yellow and Red.
**Gate:** a full 3-End match is playable start to finish with correct scoring and Hammer transfer.

### Phase 7 — Meta and UI shell
`home.js`, `hud.js`. Home screen with a rock in the middle that spins occasionally, Play button that launches the curl into the match, bottom nav with Home centred and Inventory / Shop greyed out and locked. Gameplay header: yellow and red halves with VS between, avatar, username, match score, round-leader marker, and 8 rock pips per player cycling unused → on-board → used.
**Gate:** the loop from Home through a match and back reads as a finished mobile game shell.

### Phase 8 — Art pass (Layer AI)
Asset manifest in §7. Upload `Golf Clash Visual Style Reference.png` and both Neon2 references as style guides, run `estimate_forge_price`, **bring the cost back for approval before generating**, then generate, integrate and iterate. Programmer art is used up to this point so gameplay is settled before art is committed.
**Gate:** the game reads as the stylized smooth-3D, ice-cold direction in the references.

### Phase 9 — Tuning, polish, ship
Full tuning sweep with the panel, exported defaults committed. Mobile pass: safe areas, `touch-action: none`, 60 fps on device, pointer events with `{ passive: false }`. Analytics events. README. Note on CapacitorJS readiness for the later haptics milestone.
**Gate:** tuned, smooth on a real phone, and the tuned values live in the repo rather than in `localStorage`.

## 6. How each phase gets verified

- **Preview server** — `.claude/launch.json` on port 8098, no-cache dev server, driven through the in-app browser with screenshots at each gate.
- **Physics bench** — `debug/physics-bench.html` runs headless shots across a power sweep and prints stop distance, lateral deflection and collision outcomes as tables. Friction and curl get calibrated against numbers, not vibes; this is also how "Power L3 reaches the back of the House" is held true as tuning changes.
- **Geometry check** — top-down debug view with a metre grid, compared against `Curling Sheet Reference.png` and the R1 figures in §2.
- **Rules check** — a scripted set of board states exercising each scoring and Hammer case: blank End, tie-break, hog-line removal with and without a strike, back-line removal, side-line touch, measurement threshold.

Two notes for next time. The rock was generated **without its handle**, as a separate piece, because a baked-in handle cannot spin — and handle rotation is the player's only read on which way the rock is curling. And every sprite is optional: `sprites.js` probes each file and the drawing code falls back to its procedural version, so a missing or bad asset degrades instead of breaking. Two runs failed provider-side (a timeout, and a content-filter false positive on the ice texture); both were re-run, the texture on FLUX.1 [dev] with native tileability.

Still on programmer art, and fine as-is: the UI kit and the header plates. The hand-drawn canvas UI reads cleanly, and swapping it in is a self-contained follow-up.

Design choices in the Initial Version that keep these cheap: rocks are already data-driven with a stats block and an empty effect slot; zones and per-rock modifiers have hooks in `physics.js`; the brushing controller is a discrete input layer a bot can drive; and match state is serialisable, which is what netcode will need.

## 9. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Curling's real friction curve is subtle; a wrong feel makes every shot unsatisfying | Two-term friction model with a numeric bench, calibrated before any UI is layered on |
| Perspective projection can make distance-judging feel unfair | Top-down debug view for ground truth; horizon and width-ratio sliders; the Shot Area is the player's actual distance cue |
| Shot Area variance can read as the game cheating | Variance perturbs the launch itself, never teleports the rock; the shrinking circle always tracks the true predicted rest point |
| Brushing plus curl plus collisions is a wide tuning space | Every term sliderised in one registry with JSON export, so a good configuration can be captured and diffed |
| Layer AI cost | Manifest and price estimate approved before any generation |
| Hot-seat makes solo iteration slow | The physics bench and tuning panel are the iteration loop; full matches are for rules verification |
